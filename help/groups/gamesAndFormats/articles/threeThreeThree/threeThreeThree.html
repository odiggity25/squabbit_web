3-3-3 (333) Golf Format

Also known as: 333, Three Three Three

3-3-3 is the nine-hole sibling of 6-6-6: a team game where the format changes every three holes, packing three distinct mini-matches into a short round. For example, you might play Best Ball on holes 1–3, Scramble on 4–6, and Alternate Shot on 7–9. The team with the best combined score across all three segments wins. It’s a quick, varied way to test a range of skills when you only have time for nine.

The Rules
  1. Two teams of two players compete against each other over nine holes.
  2. The round splits into three 3-hole segments, and each segment is played as a different team format.
  3. Both teams play each segment according to that segment’s format rules (for example, if segment 2 is Scramble, both teams play Scramble on those three holes).
  4. At the end of the round, the results from all three segments are combined. The team with the best combined result wins.
Nine holes: 3-3-3 is a 9-hole game. On an 18-hole course, choose whether to play the front nine or the back nine before you start.

Example

A classic nine-hole split looks like this:

Classic 3-3-3 Split
1–3 Best Ball — Both players play their own ball; the team takes the better score on each hole.
4–6 Scramble — Both players hit every shot; the team picks the best one each time.
7–9 Alternate Shot — Partners take turns hitting the same ball, alternating shots.
Combine the three segment results — best combined score wins.

Choosing Your Formats

You assign a team game to each of the three segments, and you can mix and match however you like. Each segment is essentially its own mini-game with its own format and rules — the only thing that carries across all three is the final combined score.

Popular choices for the three segments include:

  • Best Ball — Each player plays their own ball; the team takes the best score
  • Scramble — Both hit every shot; the team picks the best each time
  • Alternate Shot — Partners alternate hitting the same ball
  • Chapman — Both tee off, switch balls for the second shot, then pick one ball to finish the hole

Any supported team format works in any segment, so you can tailor each three-hole stretch to suit your group.

6-6-6

3-3-3 is simply the nine-hole version of Six Six Six. Where 6-6-6 changes format every six holes across a full 18-hole round, 3-3-3 packs the same three-format idea into a single nine.

If you have the time for a full round, play 6-6-6 instead — it follows the exact same rules, just with longer six-hole segments.

Setting Up in Squabbit

To create a 3-3-3 game in Squabbit:

  1. Create a new tournament or add a game, and choose 3-3-3 as the format.
  2. Pick the format for each of the three 3-hole segments (for example, Best Ball, Scramble, Alternate Shot).
  3. Choose your overall scoring method: Stroke, Stableford, or Match play.
  4. Choose whether to play the front nine or the back nine.
  5. Set your team size to 2 players per team, add players, and assign the two teams.

During the round, Squabbit automatically switches the scoring interface to match the current segment’s format as you move through holes 1–3, 4–6, and 7–9. The combined score across all three segments is tracked on the main scoreboard.

Tip: Want a longer round? Choose 6-6-6 instead to run the same rotating-format idea across all 18 holes.
