Two Net One Gross Golf Format

Also known as: 2 Net 1 Gross

Two Net One Gross is a team format where only the best three balls on each hole count — the two lowest net scores plus the single lowest gross score. Because two of the three counted balls use handicaps, it leans toward teams with mixed abilities and rewards a steady scoring effort from more than one player. It’s a popular choice for charity scrambles’ low-key cousin: friendly, team-based, and forgiving of a bad hole or two.

The Rules
  1. Every player plays their own ball on every hole — no picking a single best shot like a scramble.
  2. On each hole, take the two lowest net scores from two different team members.
  3. Then add the one lowest gross score from a third, different team member.
  4. Sum those three scores to get the team’s score for the hole.
  5. The team with the lowest 18-hole total wins.
Three different players: The three counted balls — the two net and the one gross — must each come from a different team member, so you need at least three players on a team. Two Net One Gross is the net-heavy sibling of Two Gross One Net: because two of the three counted balls are net scores, it tilts in favor of higher-handicap play.

Example Hole

A four-person team is playing a par 4. Each player posts a gross score, and the one or two handicap strokes they get on this hole turn it into a net score:

Par 4 — Hole Scores
A Gross 4, net 4 (no stroke)
B Gross 5, net 4 (one stroke)
C Gross 6, net 4 (two strokes)
D Gross 5, net 5 (no stroke)
Two lowest net: B (4) + C (4) — from two different players. One lowest gross from a third player: A (4). Team hole score: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.

Player A’s gross 4 is the lowest raw score on the hole, so it becomes the gross ball. Players B and C supply the two lowest net scores, each a net 4. Three different players contribute, and their scores add up to the team’s 12 for the hole.

Scoring Options

Two Net One Gross supports the following scoring methods:

Stroke Play (default)

The team’s counted scores are added up over all 18 holes. Lowest total wins. This is the standard and most common way to score Two Net One Gross.

Stableford

Instead of counting strokes, each counted ball earns points based on its score relative to par, and the team’s points are tallied per hole. Higher point total wins. This can speed up play since a player can pick up once they can no longer help the team score on a hole.

Match Play (VS games only)

When playing team vs team, each hole is won, lost, or tied. The team that wins the most holes wins the match. This option is only available when playing a VS game.

Setting Up in Squabbit

To create a Two Net One Gross game in Squabbit:

  1. Create a new tournament or add a game.
  2. Under format, choose Two Net One Gross.
  3. Set your team size to at least 3 players. The most common setup is 4 players per team.
  4. Optionally change the scoring type from Stroke Play to Stableford.
  5. Add players and assign them to teams.

During the round, each player enters their own score on every hole. Squabbit automatically works out the two lowest net balls and the one lowest gross ball from three different players, then adds them up for the team’s hole score.

Note: Because two of the three counted balls are net scores, each player’s handicap matters — make sure handicaps are set correctly before the round so the net balls are calculated right.
