TILT Golf Game

Also known as: No Laying Up TILT

TILT is a high-octane twist on Stableford scoring made popular by the No Laying Up golf podcast. You still earn points on each hole based on your net score relative to par, but a birdie starts a multiplier that keeps growing as long as you keep making birdies. String a few together and your points pile up fast, but the multiplier scales the bad holes too, so a blow-up while you are on a streak really stings. It rewards aggressive, go-for-it golf: no laying up.

The Rules
  1. Each player plays their own ball on every hole, just like regular stroke play.
  2. After each hole, your net score relative to par is converted into points using the TILT point table.
  3. Birdies and better earn positive points; pars are worth a little; bogeys are worth nothing; double bogey or worse costs you points.
  4. Make a net birdie and you start (or extend) the TILT multiplier, which multiplies the points on your next hole.
  5. Any hole that is not a birdie resets the multiplier back to normal (1x).
  6. Points from every hole are added together. The player with the highest point total wins.
Go for it: TILT is built to reward aggressive play. Chasing birdies is the whole point, because that is what keeps your multiplier climbing. Just remember the multiplier cuts both ways: a disaster hole while you are tilted up hurts far more than normal.

Point System

Before the multiplier is applied, each hole is worth a base number of points based on your net score relative to par:

Net Score Base Points
Albatross or better (3+ under par) 16
Eagle (2 under par) 8
Birdie (1 under par) 4
Par 2
Bogey (1 over par) 0
Double bogey or worse −4

These base points are then multiplied by your current TILT multiplier (explained below). On a hole with no multiplier (1x), the base points are your points for the hole.

Note: Unlike standard Stableford, double bogey or worse is a penalty in TILT: it costs you 4 points rather than simply scoring zero. And because the multiplier scales a hole in both directions, that penalty can grow if you blow up while on a streak.

The TILT Multiplier

The multiplier is what makes TILT different from regular Stableford. It starts at 1x and carries from one hole to the next based on how you score:

This hole Effect on the multiplier
Net birdie Advances one level (e.g. 1x → 2x)
Net eagle Advances two levels at once (e.g. 1x → 3x)
Net albatross or better Advances three levels at once
Par or worse Resets the multiplier back to 1x

How it builds

Each birdie advances the multiplier that applies to your next hole. So the first birdie makes your next hole worth 2x, a second birdie in a row makes the one after that 3x, and so on. An eagle jumps you two levels in a single hole, and an albatross three.

How it resets

The moment you make a par, bogey, or anything worse, the streak is over and the multiplier drops straight back to 1x for the next hole. There is no partial step-down: one non-birdie wipes the whole streak.

It cuts both ways: The multiplier scales the entire hole, good or bad. If you are riding a 3x multiplier and make a double bogey, that is −4 × 3 = −12 points. The higher your streak climbs, the more a blow-up can cost you.

Example Streak

Here is how a run of five holes might play out. Notice that each birdie sets the multiplier for the following hole, and the double bogey both scores badly and resets the streak.

A five-hole TILT run
1. Net birdie at 1x: 4 × 1 = 4 points. The next hole becomes 2x.
2. Net birdie at 2x: 4 × 2 = 8 points. The next hole becomes 3x.
3. Net birdie at 3x: 4 × 3 = 12 points. The next hole becomes 4x.
4. Net double bogey at 4x: −4 × 4 = −16 points. The streak resets to 1x.
5. Net par at 1x: 2 × 1 = 2 points. Still 1x for the next hole.
Five-hole total: 4 + 8 + 12 − 16 + 2 = 10 points

Three birdies in a row banked 24 points, but the double bogey at 4x gave back 16 of them. That tension (keep pushing or protect your streak) is the heart of TILT.

Handicap Options

By default, TILT in Squabbit uses net scores with handicaps enabled. Every result in the point table and every birdie that moves the multiplier is based on your net score, after handicap strokes are applied.

Net Scoring (default)

Each player receives handicap strokes based on their course handicap and the hole’s stroke index. A hole where you net a birdie counts as a birdie for both points and the multiplier, even if your gross score was a par. This lets golfers of all abilities build streaks and compete fairly.

Gross Scoring

You can turn off handicaps to play gross TILT, where points and the multiplier are based on your actual strokes. This favours lower-handicap players, who make more gross birdies, but keeps things simple for scratch groups.

Tip: Net scoring keeps TILT fun for a mixed group, because a well-placed handicap stroke can turn an ordinary par into a streak-extending net birdie.

Setting Up in Squabbit

To create a TILT game in Squabbit:

  1. Create a new tournament or casual game.
  2. Under format, choose TILT.
  3. Handicaps are enabled by default with net scoring. Turn them off if you want to play gross.
  4. Add players and start your round.

During the round, enter each player’s gross strokes on each hole. Squabbit automatically works out the net score, applies the TILT point table, carries the multiplier from hole to hole, and keeps a running point total for every player.

Note: Because the multiplier depends on the order holes are played, Squabbit tracks each player’s streak for you as scores come in, so there is nothing extra to toggle hole by hole.
