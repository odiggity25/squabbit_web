FedEx Cup Format

The FedEx Cup format is a points race: each round (or event) is scored by a game you pick, and players or teams earn points based on where they finish, with those points adding up to crown an overall champion. Squabbit offers two versions, Individual FedEx Cup and Team FedEx Cup, and you can run them either across the rounds of a single tournament or across a whole season as a league.

Running a season? Use a league, not a tournament. Most FedEx Cup competitions are season-long, with points building up across many events over weeks or months. That is a league. A tournament runs the FedEx Cup across the rounds of one event; a league runs it across every event in your season, using the very same Individual and Team FedEx Cup scoring. If your points race spans multiple events, create a league and choose FedEx Cup as its format. Pick a single tournament only when the entire race happens inside one multi-round event. (A common mistake is creating a tournament for a season-long points chase when a league is what you actually want.)

How It Works

FedEx Cup is a multi-round format. Think of it like a mini season played inside one tournament:

  1. Your tournament has two or more rounds.
  2. Each round is played and scored as its own game, using whichever scoring you choose for that round.
  3. At the end of each round, Squabbit ranks that round’s leaderboard from first to last.
  4. Each finishing position is worth a number of points, taken from the points table you set up (for example 1st = 10, 2nd = 5, 3rd = 3, and so on).
  5. Those points carry forward and are added up across every round.
  6. Whoever has the most total points when the tournament finishes is the champion.

The key idea is that the overall standings are driven by points for finishing position each round, not by raw strokes. A player can shoot a high number in one round, bounce back with a win in the next, and still climb the standings.

Different games per round: You do not have to use the same game in every round. Each round can be scored by its own game, so you can mix things up the way the real FedEx Cup playoffs mix event formats.

Individual FedEx Cup

In the Individual FedEx Cup, every player competes on their own. Each round is scored by an individual game, and the round’s leaderboard decides who earns which points.

  • Each round is scored using stroke play, Stableford, or quota.
  • Players are ranked within that round, and each player earns the points for their finishing place.
  • A player’s overall standing is the sum of the points they earned across all rounds.

If your tournament uses flights, players are ranked and awarded points within their own flight, so a flight is effectively its own points race.

Team FedEx Cup

The Team FedEx Cup works the same way, but teams earn the points instead of individuals. Like a Ryder Cup, you can choose a different game for each round.

  • Each round can be scored by a team game (such as best ball, scramble, or a best-balls combination) or by an individual game.
  • When a round uses a team game, the teams are ranked directly and each team earns the points for its finishing place.
  • When a round uses an individual game, each player is ranked individually and their place points are added into their team’s total for that round.
  • A team’s overall standing is the sum of the points it earned across all rounds.
Individual rounds inside a team race: Running an individual round in a Team FedEx Cup is a great way to let every player contribute, since each teammate’s finishing points all stack onto the team.

The Points Table

You decide how many points each finishing position is worth. This is the heart of the format, and it is fully customizable. The default table awards:

FinishPoints
1st10
2nd5
3rd3
4th1
5th and below0

You can change any of these values and add points for as many places as you want, so you can make the top-heavy or spread the points more evenly across the field. Because the points are customizable, you can also set it up so that the fewest points wins if you prefer.

Ties

When two or more players or teams tie for a position in a round, you choose how the points for the tied places are handled:

Average points (default)

The points for all the tied places are pooled and split evenly among the tied players or teams. For example, if two players tie for 1st, the points for 1st and 2nd are added together and each player gets half.

Full points

Each tied player or team receives the full points for the higher of the tied places. Using the same example, both players tied for 1st would each receive the full 1st-place points.

Example

A four-player Individual FedEx Cup plays two rounds of stroke play using the default points table (1st = 10, 2nd = 5, 3rd = 3, 4th = 1):

Round 1 finish
1Player A — 10 pts
2Player B — 5 pts
3Player C — 3 pts
4Player D — 1 pt
Round 2 finish
1Player C — 10 pts
2Player A — 5 pts
3Player D — 3 pts
4Player B — 1 pt
Overall: Player A 15, Player C 13, Player B 6, Player D 4 → Player A wins

Player A never won a round outright but finished 1st then 2nd, and the steady points total carried the race. Player C surged with a round win but could not quite catch up.

Setting Up in Squabbit

To create a FedEx Cup in Squabbit:

  1. Create a tournament with two or more rounds.
  2. For the format, choose Individual FedEx Cup or Team FedEx Cup.
  3. Open the FedEx points setting and set how many points each finishing position is worth.
  4. Choose how ties are handled: average points or full points.
  5. Pick the game used to score each round. In Team FedEx Cup you can mix team games and individual games across rounds.
  6. Optionally set best rounds to use so only a player’s or team’s best few rounds count toward the total, rather than every round.
  7. Add players (and assign them to teams for Team FedEx Cup).

As each round is played, Squabbit automatically ranks that round, awards the points, and keeps the running totals so you always see the live FedEx Cup standings.

Best rounds to use: Leaving this at “all rounds” sums every round. Setting it to a smaller number keeps only each competitor’s top-scoring rounds, which is handy when players miss a round or you want to reward a hot streak.
