Erado Golf Game

Erado is an individual golf game with a forgiving twist: every player gets a handful of “erase bullets” they can spend to wipe a hole completely off their card. Hit a triple bogey, erase it, and it’s as if that hole never happened: both its strokes and its par disappear. You’re scored net against par over only the holes you kept, so Erado rewards smart decisions as much as good shots. The lowest net score to par wins.

The Rules
  1. Everyone plays their own ball, hole by hole, just like normal stroke play.
  2. Each player starts the round with a set number of erase bullets (the organizer picks the number, 3 by default).
  3. On any hole, before you move on, you decide whether to keep that hole’s score or spend one bullet to erase it.
  4. An erased hole drops out of your score completely: both its net strokes and its par disappear.
  5. You are scored on your net score to par over only the holes you kept.
  6. The player with the lowest net score to par wins.
Net play: Erado is played net, so handicap strokes are applied on each hole. That lets players of different abilities compete on an even footing: you’re racing to go as far under par as you can over your kept holes.

Erase Bullets Explained

Erase bullets are what make Erado different from a normal net stroke-play round. Each one lets you wipe a single hole off your card. When you erase a hole, it doesn’t just zero out: it vanishes entirely. Both the strokes you took and the par for that hole are removed, so a blow-up you erase leaves your score to par exactly where it was before you reached that hole.

How many you get

The organizer sets how many erase bullets each player has for the game. The default is 3, and it can be set anywhere from 0 up to one fewer than the number of holes. Everyone in the game gets the same number. Unused bullets are simply left on the table: there’s no bonus for saving them.

The rules of erasing

  • You can never erase your final hole. The erase option is hidden on your last hole, so the game always ends on a scored hole.
  • You can’t run out and borrow. Once your bullets are spent, the erase option is unavailable until the game is over.
  • The decision locks when you move on. Once you enter a score on your next hole, the keep-or-erase choice for the previous hole is locked and can no longer be changed. You decide in the moment, not in hindsight at the end of the round.
It only affects Erado: Erasing a hole never touches your real scorecard total or your handicap record. The hole still counts as played for your regular round: it only drops out of the Erado game’s score.

Deciding When to Erase

Because the decision locks the moment you move to the next hole, Erado is as much about course management and nerve as it is about ball striking. A few things to weigh:

  • Erasing only helps on holes worse than par. Removing a hole takes away both your net strokes and its par. If you were at or under par on the hole, erasing it does nothing good: you’d be throwing away a score that was already helping you.
  • Save bullets for genuine disasters. Erasing a bogey saves you a stroke, but erasing a triple bogey saves you three. Spend a bullet early on a small mistake and you may have nothing left when the wheels really come off.
  • You can’t reach back. There’s no erasing an early blow-up once you’ve played on. If you’re unsure, you have to decide before you tee off on the next hole.

Example Round

Dana is playing an Erado game with 3 erase bullets. To keep it simple, assume she gets no handicap strokes on these holes, so her net score equals her gross score. Here’s how a stretch of her round plays out:

Hole 1: Par 4
Dana makes a 4 (even par). She keeps it: no reason to erase a par.
Running total: even par. Bullets left: 3.
Hole 2: Par 3
Disaster: a 7 on a par 3 (4 over). She spends a bullet and erases the hole before teeing off on Hole 3.
Both the 7 and the par 3 drop out. Running total stays even par. Bullets left: 2.
Hole 3: Par 5
She makes a birdie 4. She keeps it: a hole under par is pure gain.
Running total: 1 under par. Bullets left: 2.
After 3 holes, Dana sits at 1 under par over the 2 holes she kept: the blow-up on Hole 2 never counted.

Erado Settings

Erado keeps setup simple. The main thing you choose is how many holes each player can erase:

Setting Description
Erase bullets How many holes each player may erase during the round. An erased hole drops out of the score completely, both its strokes and its par. Players can never erase their final hole. Default 3
Tip: A higher bullet count makes the game more forgiving and keeps more players in contention. Lower it for a tougher game where a single wasted bullet can cost you.

Setting Up in Squabbit
  1. Create a new game or tournament.
  2. Under format, choose Erado.
  3. Set the number of erase bullets each player gets (3 is the default).
  4. Add your players and start the round.

During play, each player enters their own score as normal. On each hole (except the final one) you’ll see an Erase hole option showing how many bullets you have left. Tap it to wipe that hole from your Erado score. The choice stays changeable until you enter a score on the next hole, then it locks.

Squabbit automatically tracks which holes each player erased, drops them from the score, and keeps a running net-to-par total over the kept holes. Erased holes show as a ✕ on the Erado leaderboard so everyone can see where bullets were spent.

Note: Erasing a hole only affects the Erado game. Your regular scorecard total and handicap record are untouched: the hole still counts as played, it just doesn’t count toward your Erado score.
