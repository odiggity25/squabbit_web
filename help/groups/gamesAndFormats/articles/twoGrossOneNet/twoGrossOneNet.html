Two Gross One Net Golf Format

Also known as: 2 Gross 1 Net

Two Gross One Net is a team format that rewards both low raw scoring and a strong net hole. On every hole your team counts three balls — the two lowest gross scores plus the one lowest net score — each from a different player. It blends the straight-up challenge of gross golf with a handicap-adjusted net ball, so teams with mixed skill levels stay competitive while the best players still carry the gross contribution.

The Rules
  1. Every player on the team plays their own ball for the entire hole.
  2. On each hole, take the two lowest gross scores — each from a different player.
  3. Then take the one lowest net score from a third, different player.
  4. Add those three scores together to get the team’s score for the hole.
  5. The team with the lowest total over all 18 holes wins.
Different players required: Each of the three counted balls — the two gross and the one net — must come from a different team member. No single player’s score can be counted twice on a hole, so you need at least three players on a team to play this format.

Example Hole

A four-person team is playing a par 4. Each player posts a gross score, and their net score is the gross minus any strokes they receive on the hole:

Par 4 — Team Scores
A Gross 4, Net 4 (no stroke on this hole)
B Gross 5, Net 4 (one stroke)
C Gross 4, Net 3 (one stroke)
D Gross 6, Net 4 (two strokes)
Two lowest gross from different players: Player A (4) and Player C (4) — but C is needed for the net ball, so the two gross balls come from A (4) and B (5).
Lowest net from a third, different player: Player C (net 3).
Team score: 4 + 5 + 3 = 12

The app automatically works out the best legal combination of two gross and one net, each from a different player, so you always get the lowest valid team score for the hole.

Scoring Options

Two Gross One Net supports the following scoring methods:

Stroke Play (default)

The team’s counted scores are added up over all 18 holes. Lowest total wins. This is the standard and most common way to score Two Gross One Net.

Stableford

Instead of counting strokes, the team earns points on each hole based on the counted scores relative to par. Higher point total wins. This can speed up play, since a player can pick up once their ball can no longer help the team’s gross or net contribution.

Match Play (VS games only)

When played as a head-to-head VS game, two teams compete directly and each hole is won, lost, or tied. The team that wins the most holes wins the match. This option is only available when playing a VS game.

Setting Up in Squabbit

To create a Two Gross One Net game in Squabbit:

  1. Create a new tournament or add a game.
  2. Under format, choose Two Gross One Net.
  3. Set your team size. It must be at least 3 players so there is a separate ball for each counted gross and net score. The default is 4 players per team.
  4. Optionally change the scoring type from Stroke Play to Stableford.
  5. Add players and assign them to teams.

During the round, each player enters their own score — everyone plays their own ball the whole way. Squabbit handles the handicap strokes for the net ball and picks the best legal two-gross, one-net combination on every hole for you.

Note: Because the net ball relies on each player’s handicap, make sure every player’s handicap is set before the round so the net scores are calculated correctly.
