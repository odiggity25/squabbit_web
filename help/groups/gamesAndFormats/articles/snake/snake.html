Snake Golf Game

Also known as the three-putt game or the putting hot potato

Snake is a lighthearted side game built around one of golf’s most dreaded mistakes: the three-putt. The moment you three-putt a green, you’re stuck holding the “snake.” You carry it from hole to hole until someone else three-putts and takes it off your hands. Whoever is still holding the snake when the round ends is the loser. It’s a hot-potato game: nobody wants to be last to make a mess on the greens. Squabbit also lets you add a few more animals (the camel, the gorilla, and the frog) so bunkers, lost balls, and the water all come with their own hot potato too.

The Rules
  1. The snake starts the round with no one holding it.
  2. The first player to three-putt a green picks up the snake.
  3. They keep holding it until another player three-putts, who then takes it from them.
  4. The snake is always held by the most recent player to three-putt, so it passes back and forth all round long.
  5. Whoever is still holding the snake after the final hole is the loser.
Just for fun: Snake is a side game. It has no stroke scoring and does not change your position on the main leaderboard. Groups often play it for a small side bet or simply for bragging rights: the person left holding the snake buys the first round at the bar.

How Possession Works

You don’t have to track the snake by hand. Squabbit works out who is holding each animal straight from the hole stats players already enter on their scorecard: putts, bunker shots, lost balls, and penalty strokes. There is nothing extra to tap: enter your putts as usual, and if it’s three or more, the snake is yours.

Possession always belongs to the last player to trigger the animal, in hole order. As the group plays on, every new three-putt hands the snake to whoever made it. If two players three-putt on the same hole, the snake lands with the one who comes later in the group’s play order.

Whoever is holding an animal has its badge shown beside their name (on the scorecard and on the game’s leaderboard) so at any point in the round you can see exactly who’s stuck with what.

Live all round: Because possession is recalculated from the latest stats every time, the snake moves the instant a new three-putt is entered. There’s no manual pass-off and no way to forget whose turn it is to hold it.

The Animals

The snake is the classic animal, and it’s in play by default. Squabbit lets you add three more, each a hot potato of its own tied to a different on-course mistake. Every animal works the same way: the last player to trigger it is stuck holding it, and ending the round with it counts against you.

Animal Triggered by
🐍 Snake A three-putt (three or more putts on a green)
🐪 Camel Hitting into a bunker
🦍 Gorilla A lost ball or out of bounds
🐸 Frog Hitting into the water (a penalty)

You choose which animals are in play when you set up the game. Turn on only the ones your group wants to chase, and remember, at least one animal must always be active.

Stats drive the animals: Turning on an animal asks players to record the stat behind it. The snake needs putts, the camel needs bunker shots, the gorilla needs lost balls, and the frog needs penalties, so make sure your group is entering those as they play.

Example Round

Three players (Alice, Bob, and Carol) are playing Snake with only the snake (three-putt) in play:

Hole 3
Bob three-putts. Bob picks up the snake. 🐍
Hole 7
Alice three-putts. The snake passes to Alice. 🐍 Bob is clean again.
Hole 15
Carol three-putts. The snake passes to Carol. 🐍
Holes 16–18
No one else three-putts for the rest of the round.
Carol finishes holding the snake: she’s the loser.

If you had the camel, gorilla, and frog in play too, each would move independently the same way, and a player could finish the round holding more than one.

Deciding the Loser

Snake rewards staying clean. A player’s standing is simply how many animals they’re holding when the round ends, and fewer is better. Players who finish holding nothing tie for the top of the standings, and whoever is left holding the most animals comes last.

With several animals in play, it’s possible for one unlucky player to be stuck with two or three at once: a three-putt, a bunker, and a splash in the water all landing on them late in the round. That player sits at the bottom, and the group can settle up however they agreed beforehand.

No main-leaderboard impact: Snake is a standalone side game. The animals and standings don’t touch handicaps or your result in any stroke-play, Stableford, or team format you’re also playing. It runs alongside them purely for fun.

Setting Up in Squabbit
  1. Create a new game or tournament.
  2. Under format, choose Snake.
  3. In the Animals in play section, turn on the animals your group wants: the snake (three-putt), the camel (bunker), the gorilla (lost ball or out of bounds), and the frog (water). The snake is on by default, and at least one animal must always stay enabled.
  4. Add your players and start the round.

During the round, players just enter their hole stats as normal: putts, bunker shots, lost balls, and penalties. Squabbit figures out who’s holding each animal automatically and shows the badge beside their name on the scorecard and leaderboard.

Pairs well with other games: Because Snake is a side game with no stroke scoring, it’s a great add-on to run alongside a main format. Set up your strokeplay, Stableford, or skins game, then add a Snake game on top to keep the greens interesting.
