The Fees Catalog

Plenty of organizers run an event where they build the roster by hand and nobody signs up through the app. You still want to charge an entry fee, split a cart, and track a Skins purse. The Fees catalog lets you do exactly that. It’s a single place to set up every fee and purse for your event without turning on self-registration, so the charges apply even to players you add yourself.

Where the Fees catalog lives

Open your tournament settings with the gear icon, go to the Finance tab, and tap the Fees chip. The Finance tab’s chip row reads Settings, Reporting, Balances, Fees, Expenses, Charities, and Auction, so Fees sits in the middle of the row.

The Fees chip opens the catalog: one list per kind of money item your event can charge. For a tournament you’ll see up to four tabs across the top:

  • Fees & inclusions — entry fees and anything players pay for or get, like a cart, lunch, or a tee gift.
  • Advantages — mulligans and other named perks players can be given or buy.
  • Hole competitions — the buy-in pots for things like closest to the pin or longest drive.
  • Format purses — the money pot for a game’s overall standings, like a Skins or 3 Putt Poker purse.

Leagues see the same catalog with a Fees & inclusions tab and a Format purses tab. Clubs get the Fees & inclusions tab on its own.

The key point: the Fees catalog lives on the Finance tab, completely separate from self-registration. You do not have to let players sign up through the app to set up fees. Build them here, hand-add your players, and every charge still lands on their receipt and your Balances view.

Adding a fee

On the Fees & inclusions tab, tap Add item. (If you have no fees yet, you’ll see a “No fees yet” empty state with the same button.) A sheet opens with these fields:

  • Name — what the fee is, for example Cart, lunch, or green fees.
  • Cost — the amount. Leave it blank for a free perk that’s simply included.
  • Optional add-on — a toggle. Off means everyone is charged the fee (a required inclusion). On means players choose whether to buy it (an optional add-on).
  • Let registrants buy more than one — for add-ons, lets a player buy more than one, with a Max per registrant limit.
  • Charge per player — for a required paid item, decides whether it’s charged once per player (on, for things each person gets like lunch or a cart) or once as a one-time fee (off, like a single hole sponsor).

Tap Add to save it. To change a fee later, tap its row to reopen the same sheet; an existing fee also shows a red Delete to remove it.

Setting up purses

Purses work a little differently from plain fees: a purse belongs to a game, so you set the buy-in from that game’s own editor, and the catalog then lists it for you.

  • Format purses — a purse tied to a scoring format like Skins or 3 Putt Poker. Add or edit it from the Format purses tab, which opens the format editor where you turn its purse on and set the buy-in.
  • Hole competitions — the pots for closest to the pin, longest drive, and similar. These are set up on a tournament round, under Schedule › the tournament round › Hole competitions, and their buy-ins then appear in the catalog’s Hole competitions tab.

Once a purse has a buy-in, every player you’ve added gets charged that buy-in automatically, and the winnings are credited back as a separate line when the game is scored. You don’t have to run a sign-up to make a purse real.

How this relates to registration tiers

If you do use self-registration, fees and purses are shared with your registration tiers rather than duplicated. Everything you create in the Fees catalog is the same set of items a tier draws from. The difference is just scope:

  • The Fees catalog (Finance › Fees) is where every fee and purse is defined, with no per-tier toggles. Think of it as the master list.
  • A registration tier (on the Register tab) includes those items by default and lets you opt a specific tier out of any it shouldn’t pay. You edit that from a tier’s Edit inclusions & add-ons button, which shows the same Fees & inclusions, Advantages, Hole competitions, and Format purses tabs with a checkbox per item.

Because the two share one list, you can build your whole fee structure in the catalog first and attach tiers later, or start from tiers; it’s the same items either way. When you add a fee from the catalog and your event has more than one tier, Squabbit asks “Apply to which tiers?” so a catalog fee can be scoped exactly the way it would be from the tier editor. Unticked tiers simply aren’t charged it.

No tiers, no problem: if you never set up registration tiers, the Fees catalog is all you need. Define your fees and purses, add your players on the Players tab, and their charges flow straight onto the Balances view for collecting and settling up.

That’s it!

Open Finance › Fees, add your entry fees and inclusions, turn on any format or hole-competition purses, and you’re ready to charge a roster you built by hand, with no self-registration required. For how those charges turn into payments and how players settle up, see Collecting Fees and Settling Up. If you get stuck or want to suggest an improvement, use the Help menu in the app.
